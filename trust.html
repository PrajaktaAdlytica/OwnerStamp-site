Three boundaries

Keep the record
honest about its reach.

Synthetic demo data.

Fictional entities and sample IDs are labeled in every demo view. Reset clears the local interaction state.

Human review.

Assignment, expiry, and proposed revocation illustrate the workflow. They do not change real source-system access.

Agreed source handling.

An evaluation requires defined scope and documented handling before a customer extract is transferred. Public forms have no upload field.

Evidence has limits

Source-linked
does not mean complete.

Missing telemetry, uncertain ownership, and absent approval context remain explicit gaps. A record reflects only the sources made available.

Preserve the ID.

Keep original principal, role, and manifest references.

Preserve the confidence.

Make source-linked, inferred, and unattributed states distinct.

Preserve the decision.

Keep the reviewer, scope, date, and proposed next action together.

Questions, answered

Clear before
you begin.

Are certifications or live integrations claimed?

No certifications, provider endorsements, or live connector availability are presented. Names such as Microsoft Entra and AWS identify proposed source inputs and existing systems.

Will a revocation draft change access?

No. The synthetic demo only drafts a local state. The proposed workflow leaves real changes to a human source-system operator.

What should we establish before a source extract is shared?

Agree the data scope, source permissions, handling, retention, and relevant security requirements before transferring an extract.
